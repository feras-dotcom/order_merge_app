Shopify Apps for Automatically Combining Repeat Orders: 4 Options Compared (2026)

Reviewed October 23, 2026

A customer places an order, then returns later and checks out again. If both orders are still waiting to ship, combining them may save your team from handling two separate shipments.

Several Shopify apps can automate this process, including Mergify, MergeIt, MergeShip, and Triom.

While all four advertise automatic order combining, they differ in how they handle manual merges, shipping adjustments, undo options, customer editing, and pricing.

The comparison below explains those differences so you can decide which features matter for your store.

Five things to check before choosing an app

Before comparing features and prices, it helps to understand how combining orders will affect your existing workflow.

1. What happens to the original orders?

Combining two orders into one Shopify order is not the same as putting two separate orders into one package.

Some apps create a new order. Others modify an existing order. These differences can affect what your staff sees in Shopify and what gets sent to your shipping system.

Ask what happens to the original orders after a merge and how you can find their records later.

2. Which orders can the app combine automatically?

Two orders sharing a customer name or email address are not necessarily safe to combine.

Check how the app handles shipping addresses, payment status, fulfillment status, delivery methods, time windows, and other exceptions.

Pay particular attention to orders that are already being processed by a warehouse or third-party fulfillment service.

An app should also have a clear way to handle orders that don't meet its conditions.

3. What happens to duplicate shipping charges?

This is an important distinction.

A customer might pay shipping twice because they completed two separate checkouts. Combining those orders afterward does not necessarily return either shipping charge.

Some apps offer ways to recalculate shipping or refund the difference. Others focus on combining the orders and leave shipping refunds to the merchant.

Ask whether an app can prevent repeat shipping charges at checkout, refund charges after checkout, or neither.

4. Can you undo a merge, and how will it affect your other systems?

If an order is combined incorrectly, you need to understand what can be reversed.

Check whether the app offers an undo function, which situations it supports, and what happens if fulfillment has already started.

Also consider the impact on inventory, tax reporting, sales reports, customer notifications, and external fulfillment tools.

A feature that works well inside Shopify may still need testing with the rest of your store's systems.

5. What will the app cost during a busy month?

If your store only combines a few orders each month, a free plan or a small monthly allowance may be sufficient.

If customers frequently place repeat orders during product drops or preorders, additional charges can become more significant.

Compare the total cost at your expected volume rather than looking only at the monthly subscription price.

The four apps in more detail

Mergify: Automatic merging with shipping adjustment tools

View Mergify on the Shopify App Store

What it advertises

Mergify offers manual order merging, suggestions that merchants can review, and automatic merging based on configured rules.

Its listing also describes shipping recalculation using store rates, options to keep or refund shipping differences, merge history, customer notifications, and the ability to undo merges.

Automatic merging can run immediately, after a delay, or on a daily or weekly schedule.

Current public pricing

Mergify has a free-to-install option with five free merges in total, followed by $0.50 per merge.

Its $9.99/month Basic plan includes 35 merges and manual tools, but not automatic merging.

Automatic merging starts with the $29.99/month Advanced plan, which includes 150 merges per month and charges $0.20 for each additional merge. A $49.99/month Professional plan is also available.

Where it may fit

Mergify may suit merchants who want several ways to manage repeat orders and need tools for handling shipping differences after combining them.

What to verify

Confirm how refunds are calculated and issued, which merges can be undone, and how the resulting orders are handled by your shipping provider. The exact behavior matters if your store already uses a warehouse or fulfillment integration.

MergeIt: Automatic rules, manual merging, and undo

View MergeIt on the Shopify App Store

What it advertises

MergeIt offers automatic combining based on rules set by the merchant, along with manual merging from the Shopify Orders page or within the app.

It also advertises merge suggestions, configurable fulfillment settings, email notifications, and a history of completed merges.

According to the developer, most merges can be undone, and its merging process is designed to preserve sales and tax reporting.

Current public pricing

MergeIt offers a free plan with six merges per month, with all features available.

Its Starter plan costs $14.99/month, includes 50 merges, and charges $0.35 per additional merge.

Higher plans include Growth at $39.99/month for 200 merges and Professional at $89.99/month for 600 merges, with lower additional merge fees.

Where it may fit

MergeIt may suit stores that want automatic combining for routine orders while retaining the option to merge orders manually or reverse certain merges when something changes.

What to verify

Ask which merge situations can be undone and whether undo remains available after orders move further through fulfillment. Test the app's effect on your inventory, reporting, and shipping integrations rather than relying solely on the developer's description.

MergeShip: Automatic combining with a fixed monthly price

View MergeShip on the Shopify App Store

What it advertises

MergeShip automatically checks repeat orders from the same customer and combines those that pass its safety checks before fulfillment.

Merchants can choose when to enable automation and configure the window during which repeat orders may be combined.

Orders that cannot be safely handled are left unchanged for review. The app also provides merge history and a dashboard for reviewing combined orders and exceptions.

Current public pricing

MergeShip costs $19/month, with unlimited automatic combines and no additional fee per combine.

A 30-day free trial is available.

Where it may fit

MergeShip may suit stores where customers frequently place several orders before shipping, particularly during product drops, preorders, and other short buying windows.

Its fixed monthly price may be useful for stores whose repeat order volume changes considerably from one month to another.

What to verify

Check that your orders meet MergeShip's eligibility conditions and that its combining process works with your fulfillment setup.

MergeShip currently does not prevent duplicate shipping charges at checkout, automatically refund duplicate shipping charges, provide one-click undo, or offer a general manual merging workflow.

If you need one of those capabilities, compare the other apps in this guide to see which one actually supports it.

Triom: Order combining alongside customer editing

View Triom on the Shopify App Store

What it advertises

Triom offers automatic and manual order combining, as well as suggestions for orders that may be suitable to merge.

Its other major feature is customer order editing after checkout. Depending on the rules a merchant enables, customers can update addresses, change items or quantities, or cancel an order.

Merchants can configure editing windows and restrict the actions customers are allowed to take.

Current public pricing

Triom's free plan includes five merges, with additional merges priced at $0.50 each. It also includes a limited allowance for customer edits.

The $9.99/month Basic plan includes 35 merges, with $0.30 charged for each additional merge, along with 25 included customer edits.

Higher plans start at $39.99/month and include larger merge allowances and additional editing features.

Where it may fit

Triom may suit merchants who need both repeat order combining and a way for customers to make changes after checkout without contacting support.

What to verify

Review which editing actions customers can perform and how those changes interact with your fulfillment process.

Also check the charges for additional merges and edits, since both activities can affect the total monthly cost.

Which approach makes sense for your store?

The right app depends on how your store actually handles repeat orders.

If combining orders is your main requirement, compare each app's automation rules, pricing, and handling of exceptions.

If your team regularly needs to adjust shipping charges, Mergify's advertised shipping tools deserve attention.

If your staff sometimes needs to combine orders manually or reverse a merge, MergeIt's workflow may be worth examining.

If customers frequently request changes after checkout, Triom's editing tools may be useful beyond order combining.

If you primarily need automatic combining and want a fixed monthly price, MergeShip is another option to evaluate.

And if repeat orders are rare, you may not need an app at all. Packing separate orders together while maintaining their individual records can sometimes be sufficient, provided your team handles fulfillment and tracking correctly.

The important thing is to choose a process your staff can understand and rely on.

Frequently asked questions

Can Shopify automatically combine orders without an app?

Shopify provides tools for editing orders and managing fulfillment, but those tools do not provide the same automatic repeat order combining workflow as a dedicated app.

For occasional cases, merchants may be able to arrange shipping together manually while keeping the purchases as separate Shopify orders.

For a closer look at the available approaches, read our guide to combining Shopify orders.

Does automatically combining orders refund extra shipping charges?

Not necessarily.

Order combining and shipping refunds are separate processes.

Some apps advertise tools to recalculate shipping and refund differences. Other apps do not provide that functionality.

If refunds matter to your store, confirm exactly how they work before installing an app.

Are automatic merging apps suitable for preorders and product drops?

They can be, particularly when customers place several orders during a short period and those orders remain unfulfilled.

However, preorder workflows, fulfillment timing, payment conditions, and shipping arrangements vary between stores.

Check whether the app can accommodate your buying window and test how it handles orders that should remain separate.

Should I choose the app with the lowest monthly price?

Not automatically.

Some plans include only a limited number of merges. Others charge additional fees when you exceed the allowance.

Consider your expected order volume, whether you need manual tools or shipping refunds, and how much work the app will actually remove.

A free or inexpensive plan may be sufficient for a small store, while a different pricing model may make more sense for frequent repeat purchases.

Before making a decision

Use the app listings as a starting point, then test the options that match your workflow.

Pay attention to how each app handles payments, fulfillment, shipping charges, exceptions, and order records. Those details matter more than a long feature list.

The four apps discussed here offer different combinations of capabilities, and some stores may be better served by a manual process.
